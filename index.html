NSW's scenic lookouts never disappoint

Last year people made 65.6 million visits to NSW national parks. A lot of them were walking to a view.

Start the walk

Going for a walk is the most common thing people do in a NSW national park, and more of us are doing it every year. I wanted to know where everyone ends up, how hard the walks to the lookouts are, and whether people come home happy.

Visits to NSW national parks, 2008–2024/25

NPWS counts park visits every two years. The numbers barely moved until 2014, then jumped. During the 2020 lockdowns visits fell by about 10 million, but by 2024/25 they were higher than ever.

Where do people go? The 20 most visited parks

NPWS runs its parks in eight regions. Greater Sydney is the smallest of them, yet its parks get about 28 million visits a year, more than any other region. 8 of the 20 busiest parks are in it.

Where are the lookouts?

NPWS lists 794 lookouts across its parks. 43% are built viewing platforms and 41% are simpler spots on the ground; the rest are viewpoints. Blue Mountains has the most of any region (195). Greater Sydney, the busiest region, has only 55.

So I divided each region's park visits by its number of lookouts. In Greater Sydney that comes to about 514,000 visits for every lookout. In Northern Inland it is about 12,000.

How hard is it to get to the view?

NPWS grades every walking track from Class 1, an all-access path, up to Class 6, an unmarked route you find your own way along. Only 7% of the track network is easy walking, and 24% is hard going.

It depends a lot on which park you pick. At Kamay Botany Bay, 69% of the track is easy walking. At Morton, 87% is hard. And the most visited park of all, Blue Mountains, is one of the tougher ones: 37% of its track is hard and only 4% is easy.
